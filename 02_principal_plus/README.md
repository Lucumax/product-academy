# 02 — Principal+

## Purpose

The Principal+ module addresses the transition that is most poorly served by existing product management literature: the move from Senior PM to Principal PM, Director, VP Product, and CPO. Most PM frameworks assume a Senior PM scope (single product, single team, tactical execution). At the Principal level and above, the job changes in ways that are not just "more of the same with more authority."

This module fills the gap between "how to be a good PM" (which has extensive coverage) and "how to be a product executive" (which has some coverage) with specific, actionable guidance on the capabilities, mindsets, and practices that change as product leaders advance.

## What Changes at Principal+

The core shift at each level:

| Level | Key change | What's different |
|-------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Senior PM** | Tactical ownership | Owns a product or feature area; manages a team; executes against a roadmap |
| **Principal PM** | Strategic ownership | Owns a product or domain; defines strategy, not just executes it; influences without authority across teams; operates under ambiguity where the problem and solution are both undefined |
| **Director** | Organizational ownership | Owns multiple products or a product line; manages PMs; builds organizational capability; allocates resources across products |
| **VP Product** | Portfolio ownership | Owns the product portfolio; sets product strategy at company level; interfaces with CEO, board, and other C-suite; builds and leads the product organization |
| **CPO** | Company ownership | Product strategy IS company strategy; reports to CEO; sits on executive team; accountable for product outcomes at the company level |

## Key Themes

1. **Authority shifts from positional to relational.** A Senior PM has positional authority over their team. A Principal PM must influence without authority. A CPO has positional authority again, but it is different — the CPO's authority depends on CEO trust and demonstrated judgment, not org chart position.

2. **The time horizon extends.** Senior PMs think in quarters. Principal PMs think in half-years. Directors think in years. VPs and CPOs think in 2-5 year horizons. The further out you look, the more ambiguous the information and the more consequential the decisions.

3. **The unit of value changes.** Senior PMs deliver features. Principal PMs deliver product strategies. Directors deliver organizational capability. VPs and CPOs deliver company-level outcomes.

4. **The failure modes escalate.** A Senior PM's failure is a bad feature. A Principal PM's failure is a bad strategy. A Director's failure is a broken organization. A CPO's failure is an existential company threat. The cost of failure scales with the level, but the feedback loops get longer — a CPO may not know they failed for years.

5. **Communication becomes the job.** At Senior PM level, execution is the job. At Principal+ levels, communication IS execution. Strategy that is not communicated is not strategy. Decisions that are not explained are not decisions. Alignment that is not maintained is not alignment.

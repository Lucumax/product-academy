# 08 — Contradictions

## Purpose

Product leadership is not about finding the one right answer. It is about navigating persistent tensions that do not resolve. The contradictions register documents the most important of these tensions — the ones where reasonable, experienced practitioners disagree, where context determines the right call, and where both positions carry genuine evidence and genuine risk.

This module serves three functions:

1. **Map the territory.** What are the enduring disagreements in product leadership? Where do strong arguments exist on both sides?
2. **Train judgment.** Each contradiction includes context conditions, reversal signals, and failure modes — the material needed to develop situational awareness rather than rote application.
3. **Document evolution.** Walter's provisional position on each contradiction is tracked over time, creating a record of how his thinking evolves with experience and evidence.

## How to Read a Contradiction

Each entry in the register is structured as follows:

- **contradiction_id** — Stable identifier (CON-0001 through CON-0013+)
- **question** — The framing question that the contradiction addresses
- **doctrine_a / doctrine_b** — The two opposing positions, stated fairly and substantively
- **strongest_evidence_a / strongest_evidence_b** — Source IDs for the best evidence on each side
- **context_where_a_stronger / context_where_b_stronger** — The situational conditions that favor each doctrine
- **failure_modes** — How each position fails when applied rigidly or in the wrong context
- **reversal_conditions** — Observable signals that should cause a leader to switch positions
- **open_questions** — What we still do not know or what remains untested
- **confidence** — Confidence in the framing itself (high/medium/low)
- **walter_provisional_position** — Walter's current leaning, updated as evidence and experience accumulate

## Key Insight

These contradictions are not bugs in product leadership theory. They are the curriculum. The skill is not choosing the right side permanently — it is knowing when each side applies, recognizing the early signals of misapplication, and being able to articulate both positions well enough that you could argue either one convincingly. That is what separates judgment from ideology.

- Contradictions should be reviewed quarterly
- Walter's provisional position should be updated when evidence or experience shifts his view
- New contradictions emerge from case analysis, source review, and practice reflection
- The register should never shrink below 13 entries; it should grow as the Academy matures

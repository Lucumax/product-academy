# Stakeholder Incentive Map

## Purpose

A stakeholder incentive map identifies who has power over, interest in, or will be affected by a product decision — and what each stakeholder actually wants, fears, and is incentivized to do. It is the foundation for stakeholder strategy: how to communicate, negotiate, and align the people whose support you need (or whose resistance you need to manage).

Unlike a simple stakeholder matrix (which maps power and interest), an incentive map goes deeper into WHY each stakeholder holds their position — their compensation structure, career incentives, identity investments, and past experiences that shape their behavior.

## When to Use

- Before making a decision that requires cross-functional alignment
- When you anticipate resistance and need to understand its source
- Before presenting a recommendation to executives
- When organizational dynamics are blocking progress and you need to diagnose why
- During organizational change (reorganization, strategy shift, new leadership)
- As a regular practice before major stakeholder communications

## Template Structure

### 1. The Decision or Initiative

What decision, strategy, or initiative are you mapping stakeholders for? Be specific.

### 2. Stakeholder Map

For each stakeholder, complete the following profile:

**Stakeholder profiles should include:**

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Name and Role** | Who they are |
| **Formal Authority** | What decision rights or resource control they have relevant to this decision |
| **Informal Influence** | How they influence outcomes beyond their formal authority (relationships, reputation, expertise, tenure) |
| **Stated Goal** | What they say they want (the public position) |
| **Actual Goal** | What they actually want (may differ from stated goal — inferred from behavior, incentives, history) |
| **Primary Incentive** | What are they measured on? What affects their compensation, promotion, or career? |
| **What They Fear** | What bad outcome are they trying to avoid? (Loss of control, loss of credibility, team attrition, being blamed) |
| **What They Gain if This Succeeds** | How does your success benefit them? |
| **What They Lose if This Succeeds** | What does your success cost them? (Budget, headcount, autonomy, visibility, credibility) |
| **Past Behavior** | How have they acted in similar situations? What patterns can you expect? |
| **Likely Position** | Support / Neutral / Skeptical / Opposed — and WHY |
| **Influence on Outcome** | Critical (can block) / High (strong influence) / Medium / Low |

### 3. Coalition Map

Map how stakeholders relate to each other:
- **Alliances:** Who naturally aligns with whom? Why?
- **Conflicts:** Who is in tension with whom? Why?
- **Influence relationships:** Who influences whom? (e.g., "The CTO's opinion heavily influences the CEO on technical decisions.")
- **Unaligned stakeholders:** Who could go either way? What would shift them?

Visual form: Draw the stakeholders as nodes. Draw lines for alliances (solid), tensions (dashed), and influence (arrows). The person with the most arrows pointing TO them is the most influential — focus your communication there.

### 4. What Each Stakeholder Needs

For each key stakeholder, what do they need from you to move from their current position to support (or at least not oppose)?

- **Information:** What do they need to know that they don't currently know?
- **Reassurance:** What fear needs to be addressed?
- **Involvement:** How should they be involved in the decision? (Decide, Consult, Inform — see RACI)
- **Win:** What can they claim as a win from this decision? (How does it serve their interests?)
- **Timing:** When should they be engaged? (Early to shape the decision, or late to ratify?)

### 5. Communication Strategy

- **Sequence:** In what order will you engage stakeholders? (Start with allies to build momentum? Start with critics to address concerns early? Start with the decider to get direction?)
- **Message by stakeholder:** What is the core message for each stakeholder? (One sentence each, framed in terms of THEIR interests)
- **Channel:** How will you communicate? (1:1 meeting, group presentation, written memo, informal conversation)
- **Feedback loops:** How will you incorporate stakeholder input? What input will you accept vs. acknowledge and decline?

### 6. Resistance Management

For stakeholders likely to oppose:
- **What is the source of resistance?** (Genuine disagreement, incentive misalignment, fear, loss of status, past negative experience)
- **Can it be addressed?** If yes, how? If no, how will you manage it?
- **What is their BATNA?** (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement — what can they do if they don't get what they want?)
- **What is the cost of overriding them?** (If you proceed without their support, what do you lose?)

## Filled Example: FinClear Q3 Resource Allocation Stakeholder Map

### 1. The Decision
Whether to defer the CTO's platform migration to Q4 in favor of PSD3 regulatory compliance and RegionalOne feature delivery.

### 2. Stakeholder Map

**Sarah (CEO)**

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Formal Authority | Final decision authority. Can override the recommendation. |
| Informal Influence | Highest influence. Her opinion shapes the entire executive team. |
| Stated Goal | "Do what's best for the company." |
| Actual Goal | Survive to Series B. Avoid concentration risk from RegionalOne. Keep the executive team aligned. |
| Primary Incentive | Company survival and growth. Board confidence. Series B valuation. |
| What They Fear | Losing RegionalOne (35% ARR). Missing a regulatory deadline. Executive team dysfunction. |
| Gains if Succeeds | Compliance met, RegionalOne retained, migration committed — all three fires managed. |
| Loses if Succeeds | Nothing directly. May need to manage CTO disappointment. |
| Past Behavior | Has over-prioritized RegionalOne demands in the past. Has deferred migration twice before. |
| Likely Position | Support (if the trade-off logic is clear and CTO is managed) |
| Influence on Outcome | Critical — final decision maker |

**Marcus (CTO)**

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Formal Authority | Controls engineering resource allocation. His team does the work. |
| Informal Influence | Strong influence on technical decisions. CEO trusts his technical judgment. |
| Stated Goal | "We need to modernize the architecture to support future growth." |
| Actual Goal | Get the migration done. Be proven right about the need for modernization. Stop being deferred. |
| Primary Incentive | Technical excellence. System scalability. Being respected as a technical leader. |
| What They Fear | Migration deferred indefinitely. Being seen as "the person who cried wolf" about the migration. Engineering team losing faith in modernization. |
| Gains if Succeeds | Migration gets a firm Q4 commitment (if the commitment is real). |
| Loses if Succeeds | Migration deferred for third time. Feels his technical judgment is being overridden by short-term business pressure. May disengage or escalate to board. |
| Past Behavior | Has been pushing for migration for 6 months. Expresses frustration in executive meetings. Has not escalated to board yet. |
| Likely Position | Skeptical to Opposed — this is the third deferral. Will support only if Q4 commitment is credible and specific. |
| Influence on Outcome | High — can escalate to CEO/board. Engineering team follows his lead. |

**Elena (VP Sales)**

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Formal Authority | Owns the RegionalOne relationship. Controls the customer communication. |
| Informal Influence | Her relationship with RegionalOne CTO is critical — she's the only one who talks to him directly. |
| Stated Goal | "Keep RegionalOne happy and close the renewal." |
| Actual Goal | Close the RegionalOne renewal. Protect her commission. Protect the EU pipeline. |
| Primary Incentive | 60% commission-based compensation. RegionalOne is her largest account. EU pipeline is her growth story. |
| What They Fear | Losing RegionalOne. Losing EU deals. Missing quota. |
| Gains if Succeeds | RegionalOne gets a committed timeline and MVP delivery. EU compliance preserved. |
| Loses if Succeeds | RegionalOne gets MVP scope, not full scope — may not be enough for renewal. |
| Past Behavior | Has advocated aggressively for customer demands. Will escalate to CEO if she thinks customer relationships are at risk. |
| Likely Position | Cautious Support — RegionalOne is prioritized (good), but only MVP scope (concerning). Needs to believe MVP is sufficient for renewal. |
| Influence on Outcome | High — controls the customer narrative. |

**Alex (Staff Engineer)**

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Formal Authority | None. Individual contributor. |
| Informal Influence | VERY HIGH — single point of failure. If he leaves, all plans collapse. |
| Stated Goal | "I want to do technically interesting work and not burn out." |
| Actual Goal | Work on interesting problems, be respected, not be pulled in too many directions. |
| Primary Incentive | Technical mastery. Autonomy. Work-life balance. |
| What They Fear | Burnout. Context-switching across three projects. Being the bottleneck for everything. |
| Gains if Succeeds | Focused work (RegionalOne project). Clear ownership. |
| Loses if Succeeds | Nothing directly. |
| Past Behavior | Has expressed frustration about context-switching. Not actively looking but would be highly recruitable. |
| Likely Position | Support — the plan gives him focused work on a high-visibility project. |
| Influence on Outcome | Critical (silent) — his retention is existential. |

### 3. Coalition Map
- **Natural allies:** Elena and Marcus both want investment in growth/expansion but pull in opposite directions (customer features vs. platform). They are unlikely to ally on this decision.
- **Tensions:** Marcus (wants migration now) vs. Elena (wants customer features now). Sarah is the swing vote between them.
- **Influence flows:** Sarah → Everyone (formal authority). Marcus → Sarah (trusted technical advisor). Elena → Sarah (revenue responsibility). Alex → All plans (structural dependency, not influence).
- **Unaligned:** Marcus (skeptical — could support if convinced Q4 commitment is real). Elena (cautious — could oppose if she thinks MVP scope threatens renewal).

### 4. What Each Stakeholder Needs

**Sarah:** A clear recommendation with explicit trade-offs. Confidence that Marcus and Elena are managed. A timeline for when each demand will be addressed.

**Marcus:** A credible, specific Q4 migration commitment — not "we'll do it in Q4" but "migration kickoff week of October 5, scope: event system for reconciliation pipeline, team: Alex + Jordan, success criteria: real-time processing POC by November 15." And he needs to hear from Sarah (not just the PM) that this deferral is a sequencing decision, not a rejection.

**Elena:** A RegionalOne delivery plan she can take to the customer. Specific timeline (MVP by May 15). Talking points for why MVP scope is sufficient for renewal. A commitment that Phase 2 (full scope) is on the roadmap.

**Alex:** Clear ownership of the RegionalOne project. Protection from context-switching. Recognition that he's the critical resource and his sustainability matters.

### 5. Communication Strategy
- **Sequence:** (1) Alex — confirm capacity estimates and get his input on the plan. (2) Marcus — 1:1 to explain the rationale and get his input on Q4 migration scope. (3) Elena — share the RegionalOne delivery plan and get her input on customer communication. (4) Sarah — present the recommendation with Marcus and Elena's input incorporated. (5) Jordan — explain his role leading compliance work.
- **Message by stakeholder:**
  - Sarah: "This plan protects our revenue, meets our regulatory obligations, and commits to the migration in Q4. The cost is one more quarter without real-time processing. You need to approve and communicate to Marcus that this is sequencing, not rejection."
  - Marcus: "The migration is the right thing to do and we're committing to it. The compliance deadline and RegionalOne renewal risk are real and immediate. We start migration in Q4 with a specific scope and team. I need your help defining what we can accomplish in Q4."
  - Elena: "RegionalOne is our top priority. Here's the delivery plan. MVP by May 15 with core rule types. Phase 2 with advanced rules in Q4. I need your help managing the customer conversation — framing this as Phase 1 of a multi-phase rollout."
  - Alex: "You're leading the RegionalOne project. Jordan handles compliance so you can focus. Your expertise on the reconciliation algorithm is critical to this — I want to make sure you have the support you need."
- **Channels:** Marcus and Elena: 1:1 meetings before the memo circulates. Sarah: Decision memo + 15-min briefing. Alex and Jordan: Team meeting + 1:1s. Executive team: Memo + discussion at Monday staff meeting.

### 6. Resistance Management
- **Marcus's resistance:** Source: Legitimate frustration — third deferral of a project he believes is critical. Address by: (1) Making the Q4 commitment specific and credible, (2) Involving him in defining Q4 scope so he has ownership, (3) Having Sarah communicate that this is sequencing, not rejection. BATNA: Could escalate to the board (unlikely but possible). Cost of overriding: If he disengages, engineering team loses technical leadership on the migration.
- **Elena's possible resistance:** Source: Concern that MVP scope won't satisfy RegionalOne. Address by: (1) Pre-briefing her on the customer messaging, (2) Committing to Phase 2 on the roadmap, (3) Offering to join the customer conversation if needed. BATNA: Could escalate to Sarah claiming the plan threatens the renewal. Cost of overriding: If she communicates poorly to the customer, the relationship is damaged regardless of what we build.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Confusing stated goals with actual goals.** Everyone says they want "what's best for the company." What they actually want is shaped by their incentives, fears, and identity. Look at what people DO, not what they SAY.
2. **Ignoring informal influence.** The person with the most formal authority is not always the person with the most influence. Map who influences whom, not just who reports to whom.
3. **Treating stakeholders as obstacles.** The goal is not to "manage" stakeholders like problems to be solved. It's to understand their perspective, address their legitimate concerns, and align their interests with the decision when possible.
4. **Communicating to all stakeholders the same way.** The message that convinces the CEO ("this protects our largest revenue source") may trigger the CTO ("you're sacrificing technical excellence for short-term revenue"). Tailor the message.
5. **Ignoring silent stakeholders.** The person with no formal authority but critical expertise (like Alex in the example) may be the most important stakeholder of all. Losing them could be catastrophic. Their position matters even if they don't have a vote.
6. **One-time mapping.** Stakeholder positions shift as situations evolve. Update the map when conditions change.

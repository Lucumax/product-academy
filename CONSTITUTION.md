# Product Leadership Academy Constitution

**Version:** v0.1.0
**Ratified:** 2026-08-01
**Last Amended:** 2026-08-01

---

## Preamble

The Product Leadership Academy exists to close the gap between how product leadership is practiced and how it is taught. Most product leadership education offers frameworks without evidence, principles without context, and career advice without doctrine. The Academy is the counterweight: an evidence-backed system of doctrine, cases, frameworks, and tools built for product leaders who make consequential decisions under irreducible uncertainty.

The Academy does not issue credentials. It does not optimize for memorization or vocabulary. It exists to improve the quality of product decisions made by the people who study it. Its success is measured not by completion rates but by whether graduates make better decisions, kill bad initiatives faster, allocate resources more wisely, and build products that create durable value.

---

## Article I: Mission and Identity

### Section 1.1: Mission Statement

The Product Leadership Academy is an evidence-backed education system for product leaders from Senior PM through CPO. It provides:

1. **Reference doctrine** — Canonical principles, contradictions, and frameworks that product leaders can consult before, during, and after consequential decisions.
2. **Practice environment** — Cases, simulations, scenario drills, and scored self-assessments for deliberate practice in judgment under uncertainty.
3. **Personal application** — Templates, maps, and protocols for applying doctrine to real portfolios and initiatives.
4. **Integration layer** — Connection points to Product Forge, VSH, Ops Hub, and Hermes for translating learning into execution.

### Section 1.2: What the Academy Is Not

The Academy is not an execution operating system. It does not replace:

- **Product Forge** — Evidence-to-backlog execution. The Academy teaches problem selection; Product Forge converts selected problems into validated backlog items.
- **VSH** — Verified software implementation. The Academy teaches technical fluency and architecture judgment; VSH produces verifiable software.
- **Ops Hub** — Portfolio-level prioritization. The Academy teaches resource allocation doctrine; Ops Hub operationalizes portfolio decisions.
- **Hermes** — Bounded campaign orchestration. The Academy teaches strategic sequencing; Hermes orchestrates campaigns across repositories.

The Academy does not provide: software engineering instruction, general management training, career coaching, venture capital advice, sales or marketing operations, people management fundamentals, certification, or an LMS.

### Section 1.3: Scope Boundaries

The Academy is bounded by the scope defined in `SCOPE.md`. Any content or feature that falls outside that scope requires a constitutional amendment. The scope boundary exists to prevent the Academy from becoming a general-purpose business library — a fate that would dilute its doctrinal coherence and reduce its practical utility.

## Article II: Core Operating Principles

### Principle 1: Evidence Over Authority

Every canonical claim in the Academy must be supported by qualifying evidence. "Qualifying" means:
- Tier A source (firsthand operator, official documentation) — single source sufficient.
- Tier B source (credible practitioner/educator) — requires corroboration.
- Tier C source (community discussion) — may support but cannot anchor a canonical claim.
- Tier E source (pending verification) — may not support any claim until verified.

The authority of a claim comes from the quality of its evidence, not the seniority of its author. A claim made by a renowned CPO without evidence carries no more weight than a claim made by an anonymous practitioner without evidence. The Academy rejects argument from authority as a valid form of product leadership reasoning.

### Principle 2: Context Over Universals

No product leadership principle is universal. Every principle has applicability conditions, non-applicability conditions, archetype-specific variations, and industry-specific constraints. A principle stated without these conditions is incomplete. The Academy treats context as a first-class component of doctrine, not an afterthought.

### Principle 3: Contradictions Over False Consensus

Some product leadership questions have no settled answer. The Academy does not resolve these questions by picking a side. It presents both sides with their strongest evidence, their context-dependent strengths, and their failure modes. The Contradiction Register (`08_contradictions/register.yaml`) is where unresolved tensions live. Students engage with contradictions, not bypass them.

### Principle 4: Decision Quality Over Outcome Quality

The Academy evaluates decisions by the quality of the decision process given the information available at the time, not by the outcome. A good decision that produced a bad outcome is still a good decision. A bad decision that produced a good outcome is still a bad decision. This principle is foundational because outcome bias is the most pervasive error in product leadership self-assessment.

### Principle 5: Practice Over Consumption

Reading doctrine without practicing it produces the illusion of competence. Every module must include practical exercises, scenario drills, or self-assessments that force active engagement. The Academy is designed for people who make decisions, not for people who collect frameworks.

### Principle 6: Depth Over Breadth

When depth conflicts with breadth, the Academy prioritizes depth. A module that covers fewer topics with genuine depth is preferable to a module that surveys many topics superficially. The pilot curriculum (Problem Selection and Resource Allocation) is the canonical example: one module, approached with maximum depth, rather than ten modules at survey depth.

### Principle 7: Applicability Over Completeness

The Academy is not a comprehensive reference on product management. It is a curated set of the highest-leverage product leadership concepts for Senior PMs through CPOs. Content that is interesting but low-leverage has no place here. The test for inclusion is: "Would a product leader make a meaningfully different decision after studying this content?"

### Principle 8: Source Integrity Over Convenience

No transcript or quotation may be invented. No paid or private material may be copied improperly. No source marketing incentive may be concealed. Every exact quotation must have a verifiable source location (timestamp, page, section, or equivalent). These rules exist because the Academy's authority depends entirely on its epistemic integrity.

## Article III: Relationship to Other Systems

### Section 3.1: Product Forge

The Academy defines WHAT problems to solve and HOW to think about solving them. Product Forge converts selected problems into evidence-backed backlog items. The boundary:
- **Academy:** Problem discovery, selection, reframing, thesis construction, resource allocation judgment.
- **Product Forge:** Opportunity assessment, solution discovery, evidence collection, backlog generation.

The Academy may reference Product Forge integration points but does not duplicate Product Forge execution artifacts. Integration labels must distinguish between "current" and "proposed" integrations.

### Section 3.2: VSH (Verified Software Hub)

The Academy provides the judgment framework for technical decisions. VSH produces verifiable software. The boundary:
- **Academy:** Technical fluency doctrine, architecture judgment, technical debt strategy.
- **VSH:** Implementation, verification, quality assurance, deployment.

### Section 3.3: Ops Hub

The Academy teaches resource allocation doctrine. Ops Hub operationalizes portfolio decisions. The boundary:
- **Academy:** Frameworks for deciding what to fund, when to stop, how to allocate.
- **Ops Hub:** Portfolio tracking, resource utilization, prioritization cadence.

### Section 3.4: Hermes

The Academy teaches strategic sequencing and campaign design. Hermes orchestrates campaigns across repositories. The boundary:
- **Academy:** Principles of sequencing, dependency management, multi-initiative coordination.
- **Hermes:** Campaign execution, repository orchestration, progress tracking.

### Section 3.5: Adjacent Repository Policy

The Academy shall not:
- Modify any file in any adjacent repository (Product Forge, VSH, Ops Hub, Hermes).
- Reference adjacent repositories in a way that implies integration where none exists.
- Duplicate content from adjacent repositories.
- Import, copy, or depend on code from adjacent repositories.

Inter-system references are limited to: (a) identifying the boundary between systems, (b) noting where one system's output becomes another's input, (c) referencing specific integration points that have been verified as operational.

## Article IV: Authority Boundaries

### Section 4.1: The Academy's Authority

The Academy is authoritative on:
- Product leadership doctrine (Senior PM through CPO).
- Product archetypes and their implications.
- Industry-specific constraints and adaptations.
- AI product management.
- Principal+ decision-making.
- Source provenance and evidence quality.

The Academy is NOT authoritative on:
- Specific products, companies, or markets (it provides doctrine, not market analysis).
- The correctness of any particular product decision in a real organization.
- Organizational politics, stakeholder management in specific contexts, or career advancement.
- Technical implementation choices.

### Section 4.2: Individual Judgment

The Academy provides frameworks, not answers. No principle in the Academy should be applied mechanically. The Academy's doctrines are tools for thinking, not substitutes for judgment. A product leader who applies a principle without considering context, applicability conditions, and the specific constraints of their situation has misunderstood the Academy's purpose.

### Section 4.3: The Walter Context

The Academy's "Walter application" notes are contextual guidance for applying doctrine to a specific portfolio. These notes are advisory, not prescriptive. They assume a particular set of products, organizational constraints, and leadership context. Students without that context should adapt, not adopt, the Walter application notes.

## Article V: Decision-Making About Inclusion and Exclusion

### Section 5.1: Inclusion Criteria

Content is eligible for inclusion in the Academy if it meets ALL of the following:

1. **Leverage threshold:** A product leader would make a meaningfully different decision after studying this content.
2. **Evidence standard:** The content is supported by qualifying evidence or is presented as a contradiction with evidence on both sides.
3. **Level relevance:** The content is relevant to at least one of: Senior PM, Principal PM, Director, VP/CPO, Founder product leader.
4. **Scope alignment:** The content falls within the Academy's scope as defined in `SCOPE.md` and this Constitution.
5. **Non-duplication:** The content does not duplicate material better handled by an adjacent system.

### Section 5.2: Exclusion Criteria

Content shall be excluded if it meets ANY of the following:

1. **No evidence path:** The claim cannot be supported by at least one qualifying source.
2. **No applicability conditions:** The principle is stated as universal without context-specific conditions.
3. **Duplicate of adjacent system:** The content belongs in Product Forge, VSH, Ops Hub, or Hermes, not the Academy.
4. **Careerism:** The content is primarily about career advancement, title progression, or compensation.
5. **Framework for its own sake:** The content introduces a named framework without demonstrating how it changes decisions.
6. **Hindsight bias:** The content presents retrospective narrative as proven causation.
7. **False consensus:** The content presents a settled answer to a question that the Contradiction Register identifies as unresolved.

### Section 5.3: The Curriculum Map

The `CURRICULUM_MAP.md` is the canonical record of what the Academy contains, what depends on what, and what assessment gates exist. Any addition, removal, or restructuring of modules must be reflected in the Curriculum Map. The Map's dependency graph is authoritative — a module that claims prerequisites that the Map does not recognize must either update the Map or change its prerequisites.

### Section 5.4: The Depth Priority Order

When resources are constrained, the Academy prioritizes depth in this order:
1. Repository and source integrity.
2. Ecosystem boundaries.
3. Principal+ capability model.
4. Pilot curriculum (problem selection and resource allocation).
5. AI product management module.
6. Regulated-industry overlays.
7. Cases and simulations.
8. Broader corpus coverage.

This order reflects the principle that depth at the core is more valuable than breadth at the periphery.

## Article VI: Evidence Standards

### Section 6.1: Evidence Tiers

| Tier | Description | Canonical Claim Support |
|------|-------------|------------------------|
| **A** | Firsthand operator (built/managed the thing), official documentation, verified primary sources | Single source sufficient |
| **B** | Credible practitioner, educator, or analyst with domain expertise; secondary but well-sourced accounts | Requires corroboration (2+ sources) |
| **C** | Community discussion, practitioner blogs, social media | May support context, not canonical claims |
| **D** | Vendor content, marketing materials, sponsored research | May only be cited with commercial incentive disclosed |
| **E** | Pending verification — claimed operator status unverified, source provenance unclear | May not support any claim until verified |

### Section 6.2: Corroboration Requirements

A canonical claim requiring Tier B corroboration must have at least two independent Tier B sources that support the claim from different perspectives or contexts. Two sources by the same author, from the same organization, or relying on the same primary data are not independent.

### Section 6.3: Counterevidence Standards

Every disputed doctrine must present credible opposition. "Credible opposition" means:
- The opposing position is held by practitioners or researchers with relevant domain expertise.
- The opposing position has at least one qualifying source (Tier A or Tier B).
- The Academy does not strawman the opposition — it presents the strongest version of the opposing argument.

A doctrine that cannot find credible opposition may be treated as uncontested, but this status must be explicitly declared and revisited periodically.

### Section 6.4: Source Marketing Incentives

Every source must disclose commercial incentives. A book author who sells consulting, a vendor who publishes "research," a practitioner whose public content drives course sales — these incentives do not disqualify the source but must be disclosed. Students must know whether the person recommending a practice also profits from its adoption.

### Section 6.5: Transcript and Quotation Integrity

- No transcript or quotation may be invented for the Academy.
- Every exact quotation must include a verifiable source location: page number, timestamp, section heading, URL with anchor, or equivalent.
- Paraphrased claims must be clearly distinguished from exact quotations.
- Transcripts derived from audio or video must be identified as such with the transcription method noted.

### Section 6.6: Causal Confidence

Case studies must declare their causal confidence using one of:
- **High:** Direct causal link between decision and outcome, with contemporaneous evidence and plausible exclusion of alternative explanations.
- **Medium:** Plausible causal link with some alternative explanations that cannot be excluded.
- **Low:** Correlation exists but causation is speculative.
- **Correlation only:** Outcomes are associated but no causal mechanism is established.
- **Retrospective narrative:** The story is compelling but there is no contemporaneous evidence — it may be a post-hoc rationalization.

Cases with "correlation only" or "retrospective narrative" confidence may not be used as primary evidence for canonical claims.

## Article VII: Anti-Patterns the Academy Rejects

The following patterns are common in product leadership education and are explicitly rejected by the Academy:

1. **Framework inflation:** Inventing named frameworks for concepts that can be explained in a paragraph. Every framework in the Academy must demonstrate that the framework structure (not just the concept) adds decision-making value.

2. **Survivorship bias as wisdom:** Extracting "lessons" from successful companies without examining whether failed companies followed the same principles. The Academy requires that principles be tested against failure cases, not just success cases.

3. **Hindsight determinism:** Presenting outcomes as inevitable given the decisions that preceded them. The Academy acknowledges that luck, timing, and exogenous factors play substantial roles in product outcomes.

4. **False precision:** Attaching numerical scores to qualitative judgments and treating the results as measurements. The Academy uses structured estimates where helpful but labels them as estimates, not measurements.

5. **Complexity as competence:** Using unnecessarily sophisticated language, models, or mathematics to signal depth. The Academy values clarity over impressiveness. If a concept can be stated simply without loss of precision, it should be.

6. **One weird trick:** Presenting a single framework, method, or mindset as the solution to product leadership challenges. The Academy presents multiple frameworks with their applicability conditions, limitations, and trade-offs.

7. **Founder worship:** Treating the practices of exceptional founders (Jobs, Musk, Bezos) as generalizable without examining the unique conditions — personal authority, market timing, organizational control — that made those practices viable.

8. **Tech solutionism:** Assuming that better technology solves product leadership problems. The Academy treats technology choices as downstream of problem selection, not as substitutes for it.

9. **Certification theater:** Issuing credentials based on course completion rather than demonstrated capability improvement. The Academy issues no credentials. Its assessment gates test capability, not attendance.

10. **Content as marketing:** Creating content primarily to build personal brand, sell consulting, or attract an audience. The Academy's content exists to improve decisions. Any commercial motive of its contributors must be transparent.

## Article VIII: Governance and Amendment Process

### Section 8.1: Governing Body

The Academy is governed by its Constitution. There is no board, committee, or individual with authority to override the Constitution. The Constitution is the highest authority in the Academy.

### Section 8.2: Amendment Process

Amendments to this Constitution require:
1. **Proposal:** A written proposal identifying the specific section(s) to be amended, the new text, and the rationale.
2. **Consistency review:** The proposal is checked against: (a) existing doctrine for conflicts, (b) the Contradiction Register for newly introduced false consensus, (c) the scope boundaries for mission creep.
3. **Evidence review:** Any claims in the amendment must meet the Academy's evidence standards.
4. **Ratification:** The amendment is ratified when it passes the `validate_academy.py` script's constitution consistency checks and is committed with a clear commit message.

### Section 8.3: Version Control

The Constitution is versioned. Major changes (new articles, new principles, scope changes) increment the major version. Minor changes (clarifications, wording improvements, non-substantive edits) increment the minor version. The version and amendment date are recorded at the top of this document.

### Section 8.4: Conflict Resolution

If a conflict arises between the Constitution and any other Academy document, the Constitution prevails. If a conflict arises between two Academy documents on the same topic, the more specific document prevails over the more general one. A conflict between a principle and a contradiction means the contradiction is unresolved — both positions remain valid under their respective applicability conditions.

### Section 8.5: Review Cycle

This Constitution shall be reviewed at minimum every 12 months. The review must examine:
- Whether the scope boundaries remain appropriate.
- Whether any amendments are needed to address discovered gaps.
- Whether any operating principles have been violated in practice.
- Whether the relationship definitions with adjacent systems require updating.

### Section 8.6: Community Contributions

Contributions to the Academy must:
1. Pass the same evidence standards as original content.
2. Include applicability conditions and non-applicability conditions.
3. Disclose any commercial or reputational incentives of the contributor.
4. Be submitted with source references, not unsupported assertions.
5. Be reviewed against the anti-patterns listed in Article VII.

Contributions that violate these requirements will not be accepted. Contributions that strengthen the Academy's evidence base, add context-specific nuance, or provide counterevidence to existing doctrine are particularly valued.

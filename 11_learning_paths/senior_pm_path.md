# Senior PM Learning Path

## Target Role
Senior Product Manager — individual contributor responsible for a defined product area, making product decisions, and delivering outcomes through an engineering and design team.

## Prerequisites
- 1–5 years of product management experience (or equivalent in adjacent functions with product responsibility)
- Working knowledge of modern software development practices
- Experience shipping products to users

## Path Overview

The Senior PM path develops the foundational capabilities of strategic reasoning and execution architecture. The goal is not to make you a "strong Senior PM" — it's to build the capability base that Principal, Director, and CPO-level development will later extend. Every capability you shortcut here will surface as a painful gap at higher levels.

**Estimated total time:** 60–100 hours over 3–6 months (can be accelerated to 4–6 weeks full-time)

---

## Phase 1: Foundation (Weeks 1–4, ~25 hours)

### Week 1: Orientation and Self-Assessment
- Read `00_orientation/README.md` — understand the Academy's philosophy
- Read `00_orientation/CAPABILITY_MODEL.md` — full capability model
- **Critical exercise:** Self-assess honestly against all Senior PM-level capabilities. Rate each as Developing, Proficient, or Excellent. For each Developing rating, write a specific, recent example of why. For each Excellent rating, write the evidence that supports it — and what a skeptic would challenge.
- Read `00_orientation/NAVIGATION_GUIDE.md` — confirm this is your path

**Deliverable:** Completed self-assessment with evidence for each rating. Identify your top 3 development priorities.

### Week 2: Problem Selection
- Read `01_core_doctrine/` — problem selection fundamentals
- **Practice:** Start a problem diary. For 90 days (continuing beyond this week), log every incoming request, idea, and initiative. For each, answer: Why was this selected or rejected? What alternative was foregone? What would have reversed the decision?
- **Exercise:** Take your current roadmap. For each initiative, write the specific problem it addresses. For each problem, rate: Is this actually worth solving? How do you know? What evidence would change your answer?
- **Peer review:** Share your problem selection criteria with a peer PM. Ask them to identify the weakest selection on your roadmap and defend why it should stay.

**Key capability:** Problem Selection

### Week 3: Product Thesis
- Read `01_core_doctrine/` — product thesis construction
- **Exercise:** Write a one-page product thesis for your area. Must include: (1) core insight about customer behavior or market structure, (2) three specific, dated predictions that follow from the insight, (3) the evidence that would cause you to abandon each prediction.
- **Stress test:** Present your thesis to your engineering lead and design lead separately. Ask: "What part of this do you think is wrong? What am I assuming that you think is false?"
- **Practice:** Identify the most important unvalidated assumption in your thesis. Design the fastest, cheapest way to validate or invalidate it in the next 30 days.

**Key capability:** Product Thesis

### Week 4: Customer and Domain Understanding
- Read `01_core_doctrine/` — customer discovery and domain mastery
- **Exercise:** For your most important customer segment, answer: (1) What is their economic model — how do they make and lose money? (2) What organizational dysfunction prevents them from adopting a better solution? (3) What would have to be true about their world for our product to be irrelevant?
- **Fieldwork:** Conduct at least 3 direct, unmediated customer conversations this week. (Unmediated = no salesperson, no customer success manager, no executive sponsor. Just you and the customer.) Document what surprised you.
- **Audit:** Review the last 5 product decisions your team made. For each, identify the customer understanding (or lack thereof) that informed the decision. Which decisions would have been different with deeper understanding?

**Key capability:** Customer and Domain Understanding

## Phase 2: Core Capability Development (Weeks 5–10, ~30 hours)

### Week 5: Product Judgment and Taste
- Read `01_core_doctrine/` — product judgment development
- **Exercise:** For every significant product decision you make this week, write a brief decision record: what you decided, alternatives considered, principles applied, and what you'd need to observe to conclude you were wrong.
- **Benchmarking:** Identify 3 products (in any category) that you believe demonstrate exceptional taste. For each, identify the 3 most important product decisions that created that quality. What were the alternatives at each decision point? Why do you believe the right choice was made?
- **Peer calibration:** Review a product decision with a PM you respect. Compare your judgment. Where do you agree? Where do you differ? Why?

**Key capability:** Product Judgment and Taste

### Week 6: Technical Fluency
- Read `01_core_doctrine/` — technical fluency for PMs
- **Exercise:** Draw your product's technical architecture from memory. Have an engineer correct it. Identify the areas where your mental model was wrong. What decisions might you have made differently with an accurate model?
- **Deep dive:** Pair with a senior engineer to trace a production incident from symptom to root cause. Write up what you learned about the system.
- **Practice:** Read one engineering design document from a team outside your area. Discuss it with the author. Ask: "What made this hard? What would you do differently next time?"

**Key capability:** Technical Fluency

### Week 7: Data and Evaluation Fluency
- Read `01_core_doctrine/` — data and metrics fluency
- **Exercise:** For your top 3 metrics, trace them end-to-end: How are they logged? What edge cases are excluded? What does movement in the metric actually imply about user behavior?
- **Audit:** Find one product decision your team made this quarter that would have been different with better measurement. Write up what was missing and how you'll prevent it.
- **Practice:** Design an evaluation approach for your hardest-to-measure outcome. What would you measure if you couldn't A/B test? How would you know if you were wrong?

**Key capability:** Data and Evaluation Fluency

### Week 8: Business Economics
- Read `01_core_doctrine/` — business economics fundamentals
- **Exercise:** Build a unit economic model for your product area from scratch. Identify the 3 assumptions that most affect the outcome. For each, determine how you could validate it within 30 days.
- **Cross-functional:** Present your model to someone in finance for critique. Ask: "Where am I wrong? What am I missing?"
- **Application:** Identify the product decision you've made recently that had the largest economic impact. Did you model it? If not, what would the model have revealed?

**Key capability:** Business Economics

### Week 9: Risk Judgment
- Read `01_core_doctrine/` — risk judgment
- **Exercise:** For your 3 most significant product decisions in the past quarter, write a brief pre-mortem: assume the decision failed badly. What caused the failure? What signals were available that you discounted? What would you do differently?
- **Practice:** Map the risks in your product area. Categorize each as: Accept (worth taking), Mitigate (worth reducing), Transfer (someone else should own), Avoid (not worth taking). Are you spending appropriate effort on each category?

**Key capability:** Risk Judgment

### Week 10: Integration Week
- **Exercise:** Take a current product challenge. Work through it using all the capabilities developed in Phase 2 — problem selection, thesis, customer understanding, judgment, technical fluency, data fluency, economics, risk. Write up your analysis and decision.
- **Peer review:** Share your analysis with a peer PM and your manager. Ask: "Where is my thinking weakest? What would you do differently?"

## Phase 3: Breadth (Weeks 11–14, ~15 hours)

### Week 11: Your Product Archetype
- Read `04_product_archetypes/README.md`
- Read `04_product_archetypes/archetype_catalog.md`
- **Deep dive:** Read the module for your primary archetype. Complete all exercises.
- **Exercise:** Identify the 3 most common failure modes in your archetype. Which one are you most at risk of? What would you change to prevent it?

**Key capability:** Archetype understanding

### Week 12: An Adjacent Archetype
- Read the module for one adjacent archetype — a type of product you might work on in the future or that your product interfaces with.
- **Exercise:** Identify 3 ways the adjacent archetype's leadership demands differ from yours. What would you need to develop to be effective in that archetype?
- **Practice:** Find a PM who works on the adjacent archetype. Interview them about what surprised them most about the transition.

### Week 13: Tools
- Read `09_tools/` — strategic tools, discovery tools, decision tools, metrics tools
- **Exercise:** For each tool category, identify which tool you currently use and which you should add to your repertoire. Don't adopt a tool because it exists — adopt it because it addresses a specific gap in your practice.
- **Practice:** Use a tool you haven't used before on a real work problem. Evaluate: Did it improve your decision? Why or why not?

### Week 14: Communication Tools
- Read `09_tools/` — communication tools
- **Exercise:** Take your most important upcoming communication (presentation, memo, review). Apply the Academy's communication framework: (1) What's the single most important thing the audience needs to know? (2) What decision do you need from them? (3) What's the risk they're being asked to accept?
- **Practice:** Write the one-paragraph version of a complex product decision for an executive audience. Get feedback from your manager on whether it's clear and actionable.

## Phase 4: Integration and Assessment (Weeks 15–16, ~10 hours)

### Week 15: Case Work
- Complete `07_cases/` — Strategic Reasoning Cases (07.1)
- **Exercise:** For each case, write your decision before reading the analysis. Then compare. Where did your judgment differ? What assumptions led you to a different conclusion?
- **Discussion:** Discuss 1–2 cases with a peer. Focus on where you disagreed — those are the most instructive moments.

### Week 16: Simulator and Self-Reassessment
- Complete `10_simulator/` — Senior PM Scenarios (10.1)
- **Reassessment:** Return to your Phase 1 self-assessment. Re-rate yourself on all Senior PM-level capabilities. Where have you improved? Where haven't you? What blocked improvement?
- **Development plan:** Based on your reassessment, create a plan for your next 90 days of development. Which 2–3 capabilities need continued work? What specific practice will you commit to?
- **Assessment gate:** When ready, attempt the Senior PM Quality Gate. The gate is diagnostic — it tells you what you can still develop, not whether you're "good enough."

## Key Capabilities by Priority for Senior PMs

These are the capabilities that most distinguish adequate Senior PMs from excellent ones. Prioritize these in your development:

1. **Problem Selection** — Most Senior PMs are better at solving problems than selecting them. Excellence here pays compounding returns.
2. **Product Thesis** — Many Senior PMs have roadmaps but not theses. Developing a thesis forces clarity about what you believe and why.
3. **Customer and Domain Understanding** — Deep customer understanding is the foundation of product judgment. Surface-level understanding produces surface-level products.
4. **Product Judgment and Taste** — This develops through deliberate practice, not passive experience. The decision journal is your most important tool.
5. **Outcome Ownership** — Define outcomes clearly, measure them honestly, and hold yourself accountable. This is uncomfortable. Do it anyway.

## Signs You're Ready for the Principal PM Path

- You can articulate a clear product thesis for your area that a skeptic would find falsifiable
- You've made at least one decision to not build something that was popular and well-resourced, and you can defend that decision
- Your customer understanding is deep enough that you make predictions about customer behavior that are usually correct
- You've designed an evaluation approach for an outcome that was previously considered unmeasurable
- Your self-assessment shows "Proficient" or "Excellent" on all Senior PM-level capabilities

## Signs You're Not Ready

- You cannot articulate your product thesis without referencing slides or documents
- Your roadmap is primarily determined by stakeholder requests rather than strategic selection
- You haven't had a direct, unmediated customer conversation in the past month
- You cannot explain your product's unit economics
- You've never written a pre-mortem for a significant decision
- You're uncomfortable with the idea of an honest self-assessment

---

*This path is a guide, not a prescription. Adapt the sequence to your specific development needs. But don't skip the fundamentals — every capability you shortcut here will surface as a painful gap at higher levels.*

# Learning Paths

Each learning path is a structured sequence through the Academy curriculum, designed for a specific role level. The paths are cumulative — each builds on the foundation of the previous level.

## Important: Title vs. Capability

Your organizational title may not reflect your actual capability level. A "Director of Product" at a 30-person startup may have never practiced portfolio allocation or organizational design at the level this curriculum demands. A "Senior PM" at a large company may have developed Principal-level capability in cross-functional influence and organizational multiplication.

**Use the capability model, not your title, to determine your starting point.** Self-assess honestly against the [CAPABILITY_MODEL.md](../00_orientation/CAPABILITY_MODEL.md). If you are proficient at your title's level in most capabilities but developing in a few, start at your title's level and focus on the gaps. If you are proficient at the next level up in most capabilities, consider starting there.

## Path Philosophy

Each path follows the same structure:

1. **Foundation:** The capabilities that define effectiveness at this level. If you skip these, everything else is unstable.
2. **Distinction:** The capabilities that separate those who hold the title from those who deliver at the level. These are where most development effort should concentrate.
3. **Breadth:** Exposure to adjacent archetypes, industries, and contexts. Prevents the "one-trick PM" problem.
4. **Integration:** Cases, simulations, and practice that force application of multiple capabilities simultaneously.
5. **Assessment:** Honest evaluation against the standard. The assessment gate is not a test to pass — it's a diagnostic to reveal what you cannot yet do.

## Common Mistakes in Path Selection

1. **Starting too high.** The most common error. A Director who cannot demonstrate Senior PM-level problem selection will struggle with portfolio allocation. Build the foundation.

2. **Starting too low.** Less common but real. A Senior PM who has genuinely mastered the core doctrine should move to Principal material rather than re-reading fundamentals.

3. **Skipping the capability model self-assessment.** The paths exist to develop capability. If you don't know which capabilities you lack, you're navigating blind.

4. **Completing the reading without doing the practice.** Reading about product leadership is not practicing it. The practice methods in each capability section of the model are the most important part of every path.

5. **Treating assessment gates as optional.** The gates exist because self-assessment is unreliable. External calibration matters. If you skip the gates, you're optimizing for comfort over growth.

## For Managers and Leaders

If you're directing PMs to the Academy:

1. **Don't assign paths by title alone.** Have PMs self-assess against the capability model first. Two Senior PMs with the same title may need radically different development paths.

2. **Use the quality gates for calibration, not punishment.** The purpose is development, not ranking. A PM who "fails" a gate but learns exactly what they need to develop has succeeded.

3. **Support the practice.** The Academy's practice methods require real work — decision journals, pre-mortems, honest outcome reviews. Encourage these, create space for them, model them yourself.

4. **Contribute back.** If your organization develops a case, a contradiction insight, or a capability refinement that improves the model, contribute it. The Academy gets better through practitioner contribution.

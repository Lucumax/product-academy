# Industry Field Guides

Field guides are condensed, actionable references derived from the full industry overlays in `06_industry_overlays/`. They are designed for use during product work — in meetings, during decision-making, while reviewing product plans — not for initial learning.

Read the full overlay first. Use the field guide as a checkpoint.

## Available Field Guides

| Guide | Industry | When to Use |
|-------|----------|-------------|
| [Financial Services](FINANCIAL_SERVICES_FIELD_GUIDE.md) | Banking, capital markets, payments, wealth | Before making a product decision that affects regulatory compliance, model risk, capital, or customer outcomes |
| [Insurance](INSURANCE_FIELD_GUIDE.md) | P&C, life, health, reinsurance | Before making a product decision that affects underwriting, claims, pricing, or distribution |
| [Power and Energy](POWER_ENERGY_FIELD_GUIDE.md) | Generation, grid, DERs, retail energy | Before making a product decision that affects grid operations, reliability, market participation, or physical assets |

## Field Guide Structure

Each field guide is organized as a checklist-driven reference:

- **Quick Scan:** The 5 things to check before any product decision in this industry
- **Regulatory Triggers:** Specific regulations that gate product decisions, with the consequence of non-compliance
- **Stakeholder Map:** Who can stop your product, and what they care about
- **Risk Checklist:** Risks you must design for
- **Decision Patterns:** Common product decisions and how they differ from unregulated contexts
- **Failure Mode Check:** The most common failure modes and how to detect them early
- **Key Metrics:** The metrics that matter in this industry (often different from standard SaaS metrics)
- **Language to Use / Language to Avoid:** The vocabulary that signals competence vs. naivety

These guides are not substitutes for regulatory expertise, legal advice, or compliance review. They are decision-support tools for product leaders operating in environments where getting it wrong has consequences beyond churn and bad reviews.

# 03 — Business and GTM

## Purpose

Product leaders at the Principal level and above must operate at the intersection of product, business, and go-to-market. A Principal PM who cannot reason about unit economics, pricing, and distribution models is operating with one hand tied behind their back. This module provides the business and GTM capability base that product leaders need to make decisions that account for the full system: product, market, economics, and distribution.

This module does not attempt to replace dedicated business, finance, or sales functions. It provides the product-adjacent business literacy that allows a product leader to partner effectively with those functions and make product decisions that account for business constraints.

## What This Module Covers

1. **Pricing and packaging.** How to set prices, structure plans, and package features. Pricing as a product decision, not a finance decision.
2. **Unit economics.** CAC, LTV, payback period, contribution margin. How these metrics should influence product decisions vs. when they should not.
3. **GTM models.** Product-led growth, sales-led growth, hybrid models, channel partnerships, marketplaces. How GTM strategy and product strategy co-evolve.
4. **Product-finance interface.** How product leaders should interact with finance: business cases, ROI models, resource allocation, cost allocation. The difference between financial modeling for decisions vs. financial modeling for optics.
5. **Product-sales interface.** How product leaders should interact with sales: roadmap communication, feature request management, custom development, pricing and discounting authority. The difference between being sales-aligned and being sales-driven.

## What This Module Does NOT Cover

- Full financial modeling or accounting
- Sales operations or compensation design
- Marketing campaign management
- Investor relations or fundraising
- Corporate finance or M&A strategy
- Detailed legal or contract negotiation

## Key Principles

1. **Pricing is a product decision.** The price communicates value, segments customers, and shapes behavior. Finance can model elasticity and margin; product must make the trade-offs.
2. **Unit economics should inform product strategy, not dictate it.** If unit economics were the only input, no company would ever invest in a new market or give away a free product. The skill is knowing when unit economics are the constraint and when they are not.
3. **GTM model and product model co-evolve.** You cannot design a product without understanding how it will reach customers. You cannot design a GTM strategy without understanding the product's adoption dynamics.
4. **The product-sales interface is the most important organizational relationship in enterprise SaaS.** Most product failures at enterprise companies are failures of the product-sales interface, not failures of product design.

## 1. Marty Cagan — "Inspired" (SRC-BOOK-0001)

### Why Selected
The single most-cited book in product management. Cagan's frameworks on empowered product teams, product discovery, and the distinction between product teams and feature teams have shaped an entire generation of PM practice.

### Key Claims Extracted
- Product teams (empowered, cross-functional, accountable to outcomes) produce better results than feature teams (output-driven, stakeholder-driven)
- Product discovery should be separate from delivery, using prototypes to test value, usability, feasibility, and business viability risks
- Product strategy should provide context and boundaries, not prescribe features
- OKRs should measure outcomes, not outputs
- Strong product companies are defined by their product culture, not their process

### Evidence Quality Assessment
**Tier A** — Firsthand synthesis from 30+ years of operator experience. However, the book is not empirical research. Cagan's evidence comes from his experience at eBay, Netscape, HP, and Continuus, plus SVPG consulting with hundreds of companies. No systematic data collection, no controlled comparisons, no peer review. The book is high-quality opinion and pattern recognition, not science.

### Transferability Boundary
Most applicable to: software product companies with dedicated PM function, product-led organizations, mid-to-large tech companies.
**Does NOT apply to:** Regulated environments (FDA, FAA) requiring waterfall-style documentation; hardware-dominant products with 18-month lead times; organizations where PMs are project managers with no strategic authority; early-stage startups that haven't found product-market fit yet (Cagan's advice assumes a certain organizational maturity).

### What This Source Is NOT Evidence For
- That the empowered team model works in all contexts (SVPG's client set skews to well-resourced tech companies)
- That specific organizational structures (e.g., reporting lines) determine outcomes
- Causal claims about specific company outcomes (SVPG doesn't publish controlled outcome data)
- That product discovery always beats requirements-driven approaches (depends on product risk profile)

### Applicable Academy Modules
- 01_core_doctrine (product teams vs feature teams)
- 02_principal_plus (product discovery, strategy)
- 04_product_archetypes (software products)
- 07_cases (comparison with organizations using alternative models)

## 2. Gibson Biddle — Netflix Product Strategy Talks (SRC-TALK-0001)

### Why Selected
Rare firsthand account from a VP of Product at Netflix during its crucial transformation from DVD-by-mail to streaming. Biddle openly discusses strategic choices, tradeoffs, and what didn't work.

### Key Claims Extracted
- Netflix's personalization strategy was an explicit bet that algorithmic recommendations would create defensible competitive advantage
- A/B testing at Netflix scale revealed counterintuitive results (e.g., larger artwork reduced engagement)
- The subscription model creates different product incentives than advertising-supported models
- Netflix made deliberate strategic choices about which markets to enter and which to ignore
- Culture (Freedom & Responsibility) was as much a product decision as a HR decision

### Evidence Quality Assessment
**Tier A** — Biddle directly led the product organization during the described period. His accounts are consistent across multiple talks and venues. However, the talks are retrospective narratives with potential selection/recall bias. Biddle has commercial incentives (teaching, advising) but the content is substantive and freely shared.

### Transferability Boundary
Most applicable to: subscription businesses, consumer products with heavy data science investment, companies undergoing major platform transitions.
**Does NOT apply to:** Products without user-level personalization data; enterprise products where individual user behavior is less relevant; early-stage startups without A/B testing infrastructure; B2B products with long sales cycles.

### What This Source Is NOT Evidence For
- That personalization always improves user experience (context-dependent)
- That other organizations can replicate Netflix's data infrastructure investment
- That A/B testing culture can be adopted without Netflix's specific organizational norms
- General PM practices outside Netflix's specific context

### Applicable Academy Modules
- 03_business_and_gtm (subscription models)
- 04_product_archetypes (consumer, data product)
- 05_ai_product_management (personalization, ML)
- 07_cases (Netflix case)

## 3. Ben Horowitz — "The Hard Thing About Hard Things" (SRC-BOOK-0003)

### Why Selected
Singular firsthand account of building a company through multiple near-death experiences. Horowitz's "wartime CEO" concept and his framing of the CEO's relationship with product provide a counter-narrative to the "inspired product leader" model.

### Key Claims Extracted
- There is no silver-bullet management framework; leadership depends on context (wartime vs peacetime)
- In crisis, the CEO must make decisions with incomplete information and live with the consequences
- Company culture is defined by what you actually reward and punish, not what you say
- The "technology debt ceiling" is a real organizational constraint that limits product velocity
- Sometimes the right product decision is to kill your core product and pivot entirely

### Evidence Quality Assessment
**Tier A** — Firsthand memoir from someone who ran an enterprise company through IPO, near-bankruptcy, and $1.6B acquisition. The evidence is one person's narrative, not independently verified. Horowitz has commercial incentives (a16z brand, book sales) but the account is grounded in specific, verifiable events.

### Transferability Boundary
Most applicable to: venture-backed enterprise software companies, companies facing existential threats, founder-led organizations.
**Does NOT apply to:** Stable, profitable companies not facing crisis; consumer products with different operational dynamics; organizations where founders don't have absolute operational control.

### What This Source Is NOT Evidence For
- Day-to-day product management practices (book is about CEO-level crisis management)
- Product discovery or strategy frameworks (Horowitz is not a PM)
- That all companies should operate in "wartime" mode
- Generalizable management advice outside the specific context of a struggling enterprise software company

### Applicable Academy Modules
- 01_core_doctrine (leadership context)
- 07_cases (Opsware/Loudcloud as transformation case)
- 08_contradictions (wartime vs peacetime leadership vs calm company approach)

## 4. Colin Bryar & Bill Carr — "Working Backwards" (SRC-BOOK-0006)

### Why Selected
The most detailed insider account of Amazon's product development methodology. The "working backwards" approach (PR/FAQ, press release before product) is one of the most influential and distinctive product practices in the industry.

### Key Claims Extracted
- Starting with the customer experience and working backwards produces better products than starting with technology
- The PR/FAQ process forces clarity and alignment before significant resource investment
- Single-threaded leadership (one leader, one team, one mission) reduces coordination overhead
- Bar raiser hiring protects culture during hypergrowth better than standard hiring processes
- Input metrics (controllable, predictive) are better management tools than output metrics (lagging, descriptive)

### Evidence Quality Assessment
**Tier A** — Both authors were Amazon VPs for 12+ years combined, working directly with Jeff Bezos. The accounts are specific, named, and cross-referenced with publicly known Amazon events. However, the book presents an idealized view and may selectively emphasize successes.

### Transferability Boundary
Most applicable to: technology companies adopting Amazon-like product practices, organizations seeking structured product development methodology, companies scaling from mid-size to large.
**Does NOT apply to:** Organizations without a writing culture (six-page narratives require significant investment); companies where speed-to-market consistently trumps upfront planning; hardware products where PR/FAQ cannot validate manufacturing feasibility; startups where the founding team makes all decisions.

### What This Source Is NOT Evidence For
- That working backwards is superior to other methods for all product types (Amazon's success has many confounding factors)
- That PR/FAQ can replace prototyping or customer research (it's a complementary tool)
- That single-threaded leadership works in all organizational cultures
- That Amazon's specific culture is reproducible without Amazon's specific incentives and constraints

### Applicable Academy Modules
- 01_core_doctrine (customer-centric development)
- 02_principal_plus (PR/FAQ method)
- 03_business_and_gtm (input vs output metrics)
- 04_product_archetypes (platform, marketplace)

## 5. Richard Rumelt — "Good Strategy Bad Strategy" (SRC-BOOK-0015)

### Why Selected
The most rigorous framework for distinguishing actual strategy from goals, visions, and wishful thinking. Rumelt's "kernel of good strategy" (diagnosis, guiding policy, coherent action) provides analytical discipline that product strategy often lacks.

### Key Claims Extracted
- Most "strategy" is actually "bad strategy" — fluff, failure to face the problem, mistaking goals for strategy, and bad strategic objectives
- Good strategy has a kernel: diagnosis (what's the challenge?), guiding policy (how will we address it?), and coherent action (what will we actually do?)
- Proximate objectives are more useful than distant visions because they are achievable with current capabilities
- Strategy requires saying no — choosing what NOT to do is as important as choosing what to do

### Evidence Quality Assessment
**Tier B** — Rumelt is a UCLA Anderson professor with decades of strategy research. The book is based on academic work and consulting experience. Examples span military, corporate, and technology contexts. The framework is analytical rather than empirical; it provides a lens for evaluating strategy rather than causal claims about what works.

### Transferability Boundary
Most applicable to: any organization making strategic product decisions, product leaders differentiating strategy from roadmaps, organizations struggling with strategic clarity.
**Does NOT apply to:** Pre-product-market-fit startups where exhaustive strategy analysis may hinder the experimentation needed to find fit; contexts where strategy must be flexible and emergent rather than planned.

### What This Source Is NOT Evidence For
- Specific product strategies (it provides a framework, not prescriptions)
- That rigorous strategy always beats emergent strategy
- Tactical or operational product decisions
- Market-specific recommendations

### Applicable Academy Modules
- 01_core_doctrine (strategy definition)
- 02_principal_plus (strategic thinking)
- 08_contradictions (deliberate vs emergent strategy)

## 6. Ethan Mollick — "One Useful Thing" (SRC-POST-0006)

### Why Selected
The most credible, practical, and research-informed source on how generative AI actually works in practice. Mollick bridges academic rigor with hands-on experimentation, providing product leaders with grounded expectations about AI capabilities and limitations.

### Key Claims Extracted
- LLMs are "general-purpose technologies" whose capabilities and limitations are not obvious — they require active experimentation
- The "jagged frontier" of AI capability means AI excels at some tasks in a domain and fails at apparently similar ones
- Prompting is a skill that improves with deliberate practice; treating AI as an intern is a productive mental model
- Organizational adoption of AI requires both top-down mandate and bottom-up experimentation
- Current AI evaluation methods are insufficient for understanding real-world product performance

### Evidence Quality Assessment
**Tier B** — Mollick is a Wharton professor with strong academic credentials, but his AI content is primarily experiential reporting and practical experimentation, not peer-reviewed research (though he publishes academically on related topics). The content is current, well-documented, and transparent about limitations. His AI newsletter has commercial potential (book deal) but the content is free and substantive.

### Transferability Boundary
Most applicable to: product leaders evaluating AI integration, teams adopting LLM-based tools, organizations building AI features.
**Does NOT apply to:** Non-generative AI (traditional ML, optimization, computer vision); regulated environments where AI outputs require certification; contexts where training data and model behavior must be fully specified upfront.

### What This Source Is NOT Evidence For
- Enterprise-grade AI deployment practices (Mollick's experiments use public models)
- Domain-specific AI applications (his focus is general-purpose LLMs)
- Long-term AI impact predictions (he focuses on current capabilities)
- Specific model performance guarantees

### Applicable Academy Modules
- 05_ai_product_management (AI capabilities, limitations, evaluation)
- 02_principal_plus (experimentation with AI)
- 12_personal_lab (hands-on AI experimentation)

## 7. Melissa Perri — "Escaping the Build Trap" (SRC-BOOK-0008)

### Why Selected
Perri's "build trap" diagnosis — organizations shipping features without validating whether they solve real problems — is one of the most recognized critiques of product management dysfunction. The book provides a framework for moving from output-driven to outcome-driven development.

### Key Claims Extracted
- The build trap occurs when organizations measure success by features shipped rather than problems solved
- Product strategy must connect company vision to tactical work through a coherent framework
- The "Product Kata" provides a repeatable method for moving from strategic intent to validated outcomes
- Product management is a distinct discipline requiring strategic, not project management, skills
- Organizational structure (product operations, strategy function) must support outcome-driven development

### Evidence Quality Assessment
**Tier A** — Perri draws on firsthand consulting experience with numerous organizations. Her diagnoses are specific and grounded in observed patterns. However, the evidence is consulting engagement synthesis, not systematic research. The solutions are prescriptive and assume organizational willingness to change.

### Transferability Boundary
Most applicable to: organizations that have fallen into output-driven patterns, mid-to-large technology companies with established PM functions.
**Does NOT apply to:** Organizations where feature shipping is genuinely the right metric (e.g., compliance-driven products); early-stage startups where speed and iteration naturally dominate structure; organizations where PMs lack authority to drive outcomes.

### What This Source Is NOT Evidence For
- That outcome-driven development is always superior to output-driven (varies by context)
- That the Product Kata works in all organizational structures
- Specific metrics or measurement frameworks
- Causal claims about company performance before/after adopting these practices

### Applicable Academy Modules
- 01_core_doctrine (outcome vs output)
- 02_principal_plus (product strategy, product kata)
- 08_contradictions (build trap vs legitimate feature factories)

## 11. Eric Ries — "The Lean Startup" (SRC-BOOK-0014)

### Why Selected
The Lean Startup methodology fundamentally reshaped how technology products are built and validated. Its core concepts — MVP, build-measure-learn, validated learning, pivot — are now vocabulary items across the industry.

### Key Claims Extracted
- Startups succeed by systematically testing assumptions through build-measure-learn cycles
- An MVP is the smallest thing you can build to test a hypothesis, not a "minimum product"
- Validated learning (empirically testing whether customers want something) is more valuable than traditional accounting in startup contexts
- Pivoting is a structured decision, not a failure
- Innovation accounting provides a framework for measuring progress when traditional metrics (revenue, profit) don't apply

### Evidence Quality Assessment
**Tier A** — Ries developed Lean Startup from his experience at IMVU and as an HBS entrepreneur-in-residence. The methodology has been widely adopted and studied. However, the book is primarily a methodology, not research. Commercial incentive: Ries sells books, consulting, and has built companies around Lean Startup.

### Transferability Boundary
Most applicable to: early-stage software startups, new product initiatives within larger companies, contexts of extreme uncertainty.
**Does NOT apply to:** Regulated industries requiring upfront specification; hardware products where iteration costs are high; established products with known customer needs; contexts where the cost of being wrong (safety-critical systems) outweighs the value of rapid iteration.

### What This Source Is NOT Evidence For
- That Lean Startup methods produce better long-term outcomes than alternatives
- That MVPs are appropriate for all product types (they explicitly aren't for safety-critical systems)
- That startups using Lean Startup succeed more often (survivorship bias in case studies)
- That established companies can adopt Lean Startup without organizational restructuring

### Applicable Academy Modules

- 02_principal_plus (MVP, pivot)
- 10_simulator (startup decision scenarios)

- That Zhuo's specific management practices are optimal (they reflect Facebook's culture circa 2010-2019)

## 14. Model Cards for Model Reporting (SRC-PAPER-0001)

### Why Selected
The foundational academic paper establishing "model cards" — structured transparency documentation for machine learning models. This framework has been adopted by Hugging Face, OpenAI, and Google, making it one of the most impactful AI documentation standards.

### Key Claims Extracted
- ML model documentation should include intended use, evaluation results, and ethical considerations
- Model cards increase transparency and accountability in AI deployment
- Standardized documentation enables downstream users to make informed decisions
- Documentation should be created by model developers, not just external auditors
- Different stakeholders need different information about models

### Evidence Quality Assessment
**Tier A** — Published at FAccT 2019, a peer-reviewed academic conference. Authors include leading AI ethics researchers (Margaret Mitchell, Timnit Gebru). The paper is highly cited and the framework has been adopted by major AI organizations. Some authors later had public disputes with Google about AI ethics research freedom.

### Transferability Boundary
Most applicable to: organizations developing or deploying ML models, product teams integrating AI, AI governance and compliance functions.
**Does NOT apply to:** Non-ML products, traditional software without ML components, contexts where model behavior is fully specified and deterministic.

### What This Source Is NOT Evidence For
- That model cards alone ensure responsible AI (they're necessary but not sufficient)
- Specific content requirements for all model types (the paper provides a framework, not a template)
- Legal or regulatory compliance (model cards are voluntary documentation, not mandated)
- That model cards have been shown to improve outcomes (adoption is still early)

### Applicable Academy Modules
- 05_ai_product_management (model evaluation, documentation)

## 17. Amazon Fire Phone Failure Analysis (SRC-CASE-0004)

### Why Selected
Amazon's Fire Phone ($170M write-down) is one of the most studied product failures from a company otherwise known for product discipline. The case illustrates how even organizations with excellent product processes can make catastrophic product mistakes.

### Key Claims Extracted
- Amazon built the phone Amazon wanted (deep integration with Amazon services, unique features like Dynamic Perspective) rather than what customers actually wanted
- Pricing the Fire Phone at parity with iPhone/Samsung when it lacked comparable ecosystem was a critical strategic error
- Internal dynamics (Jeff Bezos's personal involvement, team reluctance to push back) overrode customer evidence
- Features that seemed innovative (3D display, Firefly) were solutions looking for problems
- The failure prompted Amazon to refine its product development processes

### Evidence Quality Assessment
**Tier B** — Analysis is based on public reporting, financial disclosures, and Brad Stone's book (which included extensive internal interviews). This is not a firsthand postmortem from the Fire Phone team, but the documentation is sufficiently rich to be credible. The most authoritative account is Stone's, which is Tier A for the Fire Phone sections.

### Transferability Boundary
Most applicable to: organizations entering adjacent markets, companies where executive product intuition overrides customer evidence, hardware product launches.
**Does NOT apply to:** Day-to-day software product decisions, iterative product improvement, contexts where customer validation is already rigorous.

### What This Source Is NOT Evidence For
- That all executive-driven product decisions fail (some succeed spectacularly)
- That Amazon's overall product process is flawed (the Fire Phone may be an exception that proves the rule)
- Causal claims verified by internal Amazon data
- Directly applicable process recommendations

### Applicable Academy Modules
- 07_cases (Fire Phone as product failure case)
- 08_contradictions (executive intuition vs customer evidence)

## 18. Ken Norton — "How to Hire a Product Manager" (SRC-POST-0002)

### Why Selected
Originally written as an internal Google document and later published publicly, this essay has been a touchstone for PM hiring for nearly two decades. It defined much of the early discourse about what makes a good PM.

### Key Claims Extracted
- Great PMs have intellectual curiosity, technical competence, and empathy
- The best PMs combine business, technology, and user experience skills
- Technical background helps PMs earn engineer respect and understand feasibility
- PMs should be hired for their ability to ship, not their ability to talk about shipping
- Beware of "product managers" who are actually project managers, marketers, or designers in disguise

### Evidence Quality Assessment
**Tier A** — Written while Norton was a PM at Google, originally as an internal document for hiring managers. The essay has been cited and discussed for nearly two decades. However, it reflects Google's PM archetype circa 2005 and some specifics (strong technical aptitude requirement) have been debated. Norton now leads GV but the essay predates his venture capital role.

### Transferability Boundary
Most applicable to: technology companies hiring PMs, organizations defining PM competencies, PM hiring managers.
**Does NOT apply to:** Organizations where the PM role is fundamentally different (e.g., more business-oriented, less technical); contexts where Google's specific hiring standards don't translate; companies where PM authority and scope differ significantly.

### What This Source Is NOT Evidence For
- That technical background is required for all PM roles (debated in the industry)
- That Google's hiring criteria produce better PMs than alternatives
- Universal PM job descriptions
- Current PM hiring practices (the landscape has evolved significantly since 2005)

## Tier Weaknesses in the Analyzed Set

1. **No randomized controlled trials or systematic empirical research.** All sources are experience-based, observational, or analytical. This is the nature of the domain but should be noted.

2. **Heavy skew to U.S. West Coast technology companies.** The firsthand operators overwhelmingly come from Silicon Valley or similar ecosystems.

3. **Limited representation of regulated, non-tech, and global perspectives.** Most sources assume product-led organizations with significant autonomy.

4. **Founder/CEO perspective overrepresentation.** Horowitz, Graham, Fried/DHH write from the very top of the organization, not the PM trenches.

5. **Retrospective bias.** Most firsthand accounts are written or recorded after the fact, with potential memory and narrative construction effects.

## Recommended Additions for v0.2

1. Academic or government research evaluating PM practices empirically
2. Non-U.S. product leadership sources (European, Asian, African operators)
3. Regulated industry operator accounts (healthcare, finance, aerospace PMs)
4. Hardware product manager firsthand accounts
5. Pre-internet product management practice sources for historical context
6. Enterprise sales-led company PM experiences (counterbalance to product-led narratives)
7. More diverse organizational stages (government, non-profit, public sector PM)

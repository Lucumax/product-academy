# Navigation Guide

This guide helps you find the right starting point and learning path through the Product Leadership Academy based on your role, goal, and available time.

## Quick Navigation by Role

### Senior PM (0–5 years of PM experience)
**Primary path:** [senior_pm_path.md](../11_learning_paths/senior_pm_path.md)

**Start with these modules, in order:**
1. `01_core_doctrine/` — Problem selection, product thesis, customer understanding, product judgment
2. `04_product_archetypes/` — Understand your archetype deeply; understand the others enough to recognize when you're in the wrong one
3. `09_tools/` — The minimum viable toolset for structured product thinking

**If you have limited time**, prioritize:
- Problem selection and product thesis (most Senior PMs are underdeveloped here)
- Your specific product archetype within `04_product_archetypes/`
- The capability model self-assessment (identify your three biggest gaps, work on those)

**Assessment gate:** After completing the Senior PM path, take the Senior PM quality gate assessment. If you pass, you're ready for Principal PM material. Most Senior PMs discover they have 1–2 capabilities that need deeper work before progressing.

### Principal PM (5–10+ years of PM experience)
**Primary path:** [principal_pm_path.md](../11_learning_paths/principal_pm_path.md)

**Start with these modules:**
1. `02_principal_plus/` — The capabilities that distinguish Principal from Senior
2. `03_business_and_gtm/` — Business economics, pricing, go-to-market depth
3. `08_contradictions/` — The hard tradeoffs that Senior PMs don't face

**If you have limited time**, prioritize:
- Cross-functional influence and organizational multiplication (the biggest gap for most new Principals)
- Business economics (most Principals are underdeveloped here relative to Director expectations)
- One deep dive into an adjacent archetype (you need to understand products outside your specialty)

**Assessment gate:** After completing the Principal path, take the Principal PM quality gate. The most common failure pattern is strong product judgment paired with weak organizational multiplication.

### Director of Product
**Primary path:** [director_vp_path.md](../11_learning_paths/director_vp_path.md)

**Start with these modules:**
1. `03_business_and_gtm/` — Full depth on business economics, portfolio allocation, strategic sequencing
2. `08_contradictions/` — The paradoxes of organizational leadership
3. `10_simulator/` — Director-level scenarios (portfolio decisions, organizational design, executive communication)

**If you have limited time**, prioritize:
- Portfolio allocation and strategic sequencing (the capabilities most new Directors lack)
- Talent development and organizational design (the capabilities that determine whether you succeed or fail at scale)
- Executive communication (you're now communicating at altitudes where mistakes are expensive)

**Assessment gate:** Director assessment focuses on portfolio-level judgment and organizational multiplication. The most common failure pattern is continuing to operate as a "super-Principal" rather than multiplying through others.

### VP of Product / CPO
**Primary path:** [cpo_path.md](../11_learning_paths/cpo_path.md)

**Start with these modules:**
1. `03_business_and_gtm/` — Capital allocation, board-level narrative, organizational design
2. `08_contradictions/` — The paradoxes of institutional leadership
3. `10_simulator/` — CPO-level scenarios (board presentations, crisis management, strategic pivots)

**If you have limited time**, prioritize:
- Board-level narrative and capital allocation (the capabilities most new VPs/CPOs are underprepared for)
- Product organization design (the capability that determines whether the organization outlasts your tenure)
- The capability model at the VP/CPO level — self-assess honestly, identify gaps, address them

**Assessment gate:** VP/CPO assessment focuses on institutional leadership capabilities. The most common failure pattern is exceptional product judgment paired with inadequate board communication and capital allocation skill.

## Navigation by Goal

### I want to get promoted
The Academy does not optimize for promotion. It optimizes for capability. However, genuine capability development at the next level is the most reliable path to promotion.

1. Self-assess honestly against the capability model at your target level
2. Identify the 2–3 capabilities where you're weakest relative to the target
3. Follow the learning path for your target level, focusing on those capabilities
4. Use the practice methods in each capability's section of the model
5. Build evidence of capability through your work, not through promotion packets

**Warning:** Some organizations promote based on criteria that don't align with this capability model (visibility, politics, tenure, credential). The Academy cannot help with those criteria and does not try to.

### I want to be better at my current role
1. Self-assess against the capability model at your current level
2. Identify the 2–3 capabilities where you're weakest
3. Dive into the relevant modules for those capabilities
4. Do the practice methods — they matter more than the reading
5. Re-assess quarterly

### I'm struggling with a specific challenge
Use the module index in [CURRICULUM_MAP.md](../CURRICULUM_MAP.md) to find the module that addresses your challenge. If the challenge is:
- **A product decision:** Start with the relevant archetype module, then check `08_contradictions/` for related tradeoffs
- **An organizational problem:** Start with organizational multiplication and product organization design in the capability model
- **A career question:** Start with `13_career_transitions/` (landing product roles, credibility binder, emerging roles) and your role's learning path
- **A stakeholder conflict:** Start with cross-functional influence and executive communication in the capability model

### I'm hiring or evaluating product leaders
1. Read the capability model in full — it is your structured interview guide
2. For each capability at the target level, use the level-specific descriptors to design interview questions
3. Actively probe for the false positive patterns — they are deliberately designed to catch performative competence
4. Use the quality gates as calibration references

### I'm designing a product organization
1. Read the product organization design capability section
2. Read the relevant archetype modules — organizational design should follow product architecture
3. Study the organizational patterns in `08_contradictions/`
4. Use the simulator to pressure-test your design against scenarios

## Navigation by Time Available

### 30 minutes per week (maintenance mode)
- Pick one capability. Read its section in the model. Do one practice exercise. Reflect.
- Rotate through capabilities weekly.

### 2 hours per week (steady development)
- Follow your role's learning path sequentially. One module every 1–2 weeks.
- Supplement with practice from the personal lab.

### 5+ hours per week (intensive development)
- Follow your role's learning path at accelerated pace.
- Add the simulator for practice in decision-making under pressure.
- Contribute to the Academy — write up a case, document a contradiction you encountered, or refine the capability model based on your experience.

### Full-time immersion (onboarding, sabbatical, transition)
- Complete the full learning path for your role in sequence.
- Add cross-training: read the learning path for the level above yours.
- Do the full simulator program.
- Complete the personal lab reflection cycles.

## Navigation by Background

### Coming from engineering
You likely have strong technical fluency and may be underdeveloped in customer understanding, business economics, and cross-functional influence. Start with those capabilities in the model. Your learning path should emphasize `01_core_doctrine/` (especially customer and domain understanding) and `03_business_and_gtm/`.

### Coming from consulting/strategy
You likely have strong analytical and communication skills and may be underdeveloped in product judgment, execution architecture, and outcome ownership. Your presentations may be better than your products. Start with product judgment and outcome ownership in the capability model. The simulator will be particularly valuable — it forces decisions without the safety of a deck.

### Coming from design
You likely have strong product judgment and taste and may be underdeveloped in business economics, data fluency, and technical fluency. Your products may be beautiful but economically fragile. Start with business economics and data fluency in the model.

### Coming from business/sales
You likely have strong business economics and customer understanding and may be underdeveloped in technical fluency, product judgment, and strategic sequencing. You may over-index on what customers say they want. Start with product judgment and technical fluency in the model.

### Coming from another function (marketing, ops, finance)
You have domain-specific strengths and likely have broad gaps across the strategic reasoning and execution architecture clusters. Follow your role's learning path in full — don't skip the fundamentals.

## When to Skip, When to Go Deep

**Skip** a module when:
- You've demonstrated the capability consistently at your level (verified by someone who will be honest with you)
- The module covers an archetype or industry you are certain you will never work in (but be careful — career paths are unpredictable)

**Go deep** on a module when:
- The capability model self-assessment reveals a significant gap
- You've received feedback (especially 360 feedback) identifying this as a weakness
- You're preparing for a role transition that will demand this capability at a higher level
- You've failed at something that traces back to this capability

**Never skip** the capability model self-assessment. It is the foundation. Everything else is optional in theory but necessary in practice for most people.

## The Meta-Lesson

The most important capability the Academy develops is **accurate self-assessment**. The product leaders who improve fastest are not the ones who work hardest or read most. They are the ones who know most precisely what they cannot yet do.

If you take nothing else from the Academy, take this: you are probably wrong about your own strengths and weaknesses. The capability model's false positive patterns exist because product leaders routinely mistake one thing for another — confidence for competence, communication for clarity, activity for progress. Your first job is to discover where you're wrong about yourself.

Start with the [CAPABILITY_MODEL.md](CAPABILITY_MODEL.md). Be honest. Then follow the path.

# Product Archetypes

## Why Archetypes Matter

"Product management" is not one job. Leading a consumer social product is fundamentally different from leading an enterprise SaaS product, which is fundamentally different from leading a platform infrastructure product. The capabilities required, the failure modes, the stakeholder dynamics, the success metrics, and the tempo of work all differ by archetype.

Most product leaders develop expertise in one archetype — the one they happened to work in first. This creates two problems:

1. **The "one-trick PM" problem:** A leader who only knows consumer products applies consumer logic to an enterprise problem and fails. A leader who only knows enterprise products applies enterprise logic to a platform problem and fails.

2. **The "framework mismatch" problem:** Product frameworks and best practices are not universal. A framework developed for consumer growth products may be actively harmful in a healthcare context. A practice that works beautifully in enterprise SaaS may be catastrophic in a marketplace.

The archetype catalog and modules exist to:
- Make explicit the differences between archetypes so you can adapt your leadership approach
- Help you recognize when you're applying the wrong mental model to a problem
- Develop the cognitive flexibility to lead across archetypes — which becomes essential at Director level and above
- Prevent the most common failure modes that occur when leaders don't understand their archetype

## How to Use the Archetype Modules

### If you're a Senior PM or Principal PM
- **Master your primary archetype first.** Go deep on the module for the type of product you work on. Understand its distinctive demands, common failure modes, and how the capability model applies differently.
- **Then study at least one adjacent archetype.** If you build enterprise SaaS, study platform/infrastructure (your product may become a platform) or marketplace (you may need network effects). This prevents the "one-trick PM" problem.

### If you're a Director
- **Understand all archetypes in your portfolio.** If you lead multiple product areas that span archetypes, you must understand each archetype's demands well enough to evaluate PMs working in them and to make good portfolio decisions.
- **Study archetypes you might enter.** If your company is considering a platform play or an AI initiative, study those archetypes before you commit resources.

### If you're a VP or CPO
- **Understand all archetypes at a strategic level.** Your company's strategy may require entering new archetypes. You must understand the leadership demands of those archetypes before you commit the organization.
- **Use the archetype catalog for organizational design.** Different archetypes require different PM profiles. A PM who excels at consumer products may struggle with enterprise products, and vice versa. The archetype catalog helps you match talent to work.

## Archetype Interactions

Many products span multiple archetypes. A fintech product (archetype 8) may also be a consumer product (archetype 1) or an enterprise product (archetype 2). A marketplace (archetype 4) may be built on a platform (archetype 3). An AI product (archetype 5) may be embedded in a healthcare product (archetype 9).

When archetypes interact, their demands compound — and sometimes conflict. The archetype catalog identifies these interactions so you can navigate them deliberately.

## The Meta-Skill: Archetype Fluidity

The highest-level product leaders can move between archetypes without applying the wrong mental model. This requires:

1. **Deep understanding of each archetype's distinctive demands** (not superficial familiarity)
2. **Pattern recognition** — the ability to recognize which archetype dynamics are at play in a novel situation
3. **Cognitive flexibility** — the ability to switch mental models when the situation demands it
4. **Humility** — the recognition that your primary archetype's logic may not apply in a different context

The archetype modules develop this meta-skill through explicit comparison, contrast, and practice in applying different archetype lenses to the same situation.
